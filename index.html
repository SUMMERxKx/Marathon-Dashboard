The plan in one paragraph

26 weeks from Mon 2 Nov 2026 to the marathon on Sun 2 May 2027. Running volume goes from 16 km a week to a peak of 66, with two 32 km long runs. Lifting drops from four days to two as the mileage climbs, and almost everything below the waist comes off failure training.

Where you're starting

The engine is ready

VO2 max 58.7, average pace 4:58 /km across October. That is not a beginner's aerobic ceiling. Speed is not the limiter.

The legs are not

14 km a week, longest run around 10 km, 309 km lifetime. Race day alone is 14% of every kilometre ever run. The gap is duration, not fitness.

Every run is currently too fast. October paces ran 4:54–5:38 /km. Easy pace should be 6:00–6:30 /km — slow enough to hold a conversation — and roughly 80% of weekly volume belongs there. This is the single highest-value change in the plan.

Check your cadence

The Garmin summary reported 138 spm against a normal 165–180. That same summary gives average speed as 2.3 kph, so it is averaging gym and walking data in — the figure may be contaminated. Pull cadence off one clean running activity in Week 1. If it really sits below 160, fixing it does more for injury risk than anything else here. Strides from Week 3 are the main tool.

Target time

Don't set one yet. Run a 10 km time trial in late February (Week 17) and set it off that. From VO2 max 58.7, current paces, and the weight coming off, somewhere in the 3:10–3:30 range looks plausible for a well-executed first marathon.

Quality sessions — Tuesdays, from Phase 2

Rotate these. Always 2 km easy warm-up and 1 km cool-down.

  • Tempo — 20–40 min continuous at tempo pace
  • Cruise intervals — 4–6 × 1 km at tempo, 90 s jog
  • Long intervals — 5–6 × 3 min hard, 3 min jog
  • Marathon-pace work (Phase 3) — 8–16 km at MP, often inside the long run

Injury rules

  • Never increase weekly volume more than ~10%, and never skip a down week. Both are built into the table.
  • Any pain lasting more than 3 runs → 3 days off, then reassess.
  • Shin, Achilles or knee pain that worsens during a run → stop that run.
  • Three days off in November costs nothing. Three weeks off in March costs the race.
The one rule that makes this plan work. Keep training to failure where it is free — upper-body isolation — and drop it everywhere it costs you a running day. A true failure set needs 48–72 hours to recover from. Do that on leg press, curls, hip thrusts and calf raises in one session and you have written off two runs.

Where failure stays, and where it goes

Keep to failure

Lateral raise · pec dec · reverse pec dec · preacher curl · hammer curl · rope pushdown · rope kickback · rope crunch · leg raises

Small upper-body isolation creates little systemic fatigue and nothing it does interferes with running. Go as hard as you like here.

Stop 2–3 reps short

Incline bench · shoulder press · lat pulldown · seated row · and every single leg exercise, without exception

Calf raises especially. Your calves are about to absorb every footstrike of a 60 km week. Failure sets on top of that is how Achilles tendinopathy starts, and it takes months to resolve.

Two exercises come out

Leg extension — knee stress for little carryover to running. Standing calf raise — the road gives your calves all the work they need. Everything else you were already doing stays.

What you keep, what you lose

You will lose some upper-body size in Phase 3 — two maintenance sessions a week against 60 km of running will not hold everything. Strength holds far better than size on reduced volume, and it comes back fast after May 2. Legs get leaner and more fatigue-resistant, and lose some size. That is the trade for a marathon.

Keep creatine the whole block. Runners sometimes drop it over the water weight; it is one of the few supplements with real evidence behind it, it protects strength while you cut, and a kilo of intracellular water will not slow you down.

Fuelling long runs

New from Phase 2, and the thing most first-timers ignore until race day. Practise on every long run from Week 9 — the gut adapts to absorbing carbs while running the same way muscle adapts to load, and it takes months.

Run lengthFuel
Under 75 minWater only
Over 90 min30–60 g carbs/hour, starting 30–40 min in
Over 2.5 h (Phase 3)60–90 g/hour + 300–600 mg sodium/hour

Before: bread + banana, ~60–90 g carbs, 2 h out. After: 1.0–1.2 g/kg carbs + 30–40 g protein within the hour — the shake on chocolate milk plus a banana is almost exactly right. Chocolate milk is a poor everyday choice on a cut and a genuinely good post-long-run drink; keep the skim for ordinary days.

Iron — book this in November

Distance running drains iron through foot-strike hemolysis, sweat and gut losses, and low ferritin is the most common reason a marathon build quietly falls apart. The gummy multivitamin contains no iron at all. Ask for a ferritin test, not just hemoglobin — ferritin can be on the floor while hemoglobin reads normal, and that is exactly the state where training feels inexplicably hard.

Phase 1 · Nov 2 – Dec 27 · 172 km

The goal of this phase is not fitness. It is becoming someone who runs four days a week without breaking. Volume is deliberately low. The temptation all through November will be to run faster and further than the plan says. Don't.

Week 1 is less running than you do now — 16 km across four runs, spread over more days at a much slower pace. It should feel almost too easy. Tendons adapt to frequency and break under volume, so frequency comes first.
